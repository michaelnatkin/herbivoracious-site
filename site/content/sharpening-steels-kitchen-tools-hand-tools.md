<p>You should have a metal or ceramic “steel” that can be used in between sharpenings to keep an edge on your knife. Sliding the edge of your knife against the steel removes any burrs and realigns the cutting edge. A sharp knife is a safer knife, because you don't have to use excess force that can cause it to slip.</p>
<p>Using a steel is a lot easier to do than to describe, so I've got a <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xERHFntQzYI">short video</a> to show you how.</p>
<p>Here are two options - a {{% product id="metal-honing-steel" %}}metal honing steel{{% /product %}} for straightening the edge, or a very fine {{% product id="ceramic-sharpening-steel" %}}ceramic sharpening steel{{% /product %}} that actually does a bit of sharpening. I like them both.</p>

<h2>How to use a honing steel</h2>
<ol>
<li>Hold the steel firmly in your off hand, with the tip pointing up or resting on a folded towel on the counter. Keep it well away from your body.</li>
<li>Place the heel of the knife (the end nearest the handle) against the top of the steel, with the blade at roughly a 15 to 20 degree angle.</li>
<li>Draw the knife down and across the steel, sweeping from heel to tip, with light pressure. Use the whole length of the blade.</li>
<li>Repeat on the other side of the blade. Alternate sides for about five to eight light strokes each.</li>
<li>Wipe the blade clean before you cut.</li>
</ol>
<p>The angle matters more than the pressure. Light strokes at a consistent angle work better than hard ones.</p>

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a honing steel sharpen a knife?</h3>
<p>A smooth metal steel does not remove much metal. It straightens and realigns an edge that has rolled over with use, which makes the knife feel sharper. The very fine ceramic steel above does a little real sharpening. When a knife will not cut well even after steeling, it needs to be sharpened on a stone or by a professional.</p>

<h3>How often should I use a steel?</h3>
<p>A few strokes per side before you start cooking, or whenever the knife starts to feel dull, is plenty.</p>

<h3>What angle do I hold the knife at?</h3>
<p>Match the angle the edge was ground to, which for most kitchen knives is somewhere around 15 to 20 degrees per side. Japanese knives are often sharper-angled, so use a ceramic steel and a very light touch.</p>
