They experienced golden ages under the Byzantines and Ottomans, and remained there until the horrendous pogroms of 1947. Their Aleppo Codex is the oldest surviving document of the Old Testament other than the Dead Sea Scrolls, and resides at the Israel Museum in Jerusalem. Much of the Aleppan community has resettled in New Jersey. Aleppan Jewish cuisine contains elements similar to those found in the other Sepharic cultures, with strong Middle Eastern and Levantine flavors.</p>  <p>I tend to favor cookbooks that have a particularly strong individual point of view, and Aromas has that in spades. Dweck&#39;s book reminds me of those wonderful church, mosque or synagogue cookbooks that every immigrant congregation seems to put together after the first generation is well established, to ensure that their kids can carry on the traditions. Only this one somehow got printed in a gorgeous coffee-table sized hardback with world class photography and an outstanding historical introduction. If you like to cook with Middle Eastern flavors, it deserves a proud place on your shelf.</p>  <p>This particular soup is awfully easy to do. Basically you&#0160; just boil red lentils until they puree themselves, add a seasoning sautee and a quick garnish. I prefer to make it a little bit thinner than Dweck&#39;s version, so that it serves as a relatively light first course, but it also works in a larger and thicker portion as the main dish of a simple meal.</p>  <p><strong>Ingredient notes</strong></p>  <p>Red lentils are available at any natural food or health food store; do <strong>not</strong> substitute brown/green/black/french lentils. Instead of the cumin and Aleppo pepper, another great garnish alternative is {{% product id="pre-packaged-version" %}}dukkah{{% /product %}}, a spice blend from Egypt.</p> <div class="recipe-card">

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What lentils do you use for Syrian red lentil soup?</h3>
<p>Red lentils, available at natural or health food stores. Don't substitute brown, green, black or French lentils.</p>

<h3>How long do you cook the lentils?</h3>
<p>Rinse and pick them over, boil them in 2 quarts of water, then simmer about 40 minutes, stirring occasionally so they don't stick and scorch, until they have dissolved into a puree.</p>

<h3>How do you season the soup?</h3>
<p>Grind 6 cloves of garlic, the coriander seed and salt into a coarse paste, fry it in olive oil for about 1 minute without burning the garlic, and add it to the soup to simmer a bit longer.</p>

<h3>What garnish goes on shurbat addes?</h3>
<p>Fresh cilantro, ground cumin, Aleppo pepper, dukkah or chili flakes, with lemon wedges on the side. Aleppo pepper is specific to Syrian food and hard to find in US stores; dukkah, an Egyptian spice blend, is a great alternative.</p>

<h3>How thick should the soup be?</h3>
<p>The author prefers it moderately thin, as a light first course, but thick works as a main dish. Adjust the texture with water first, then salt.</p>
