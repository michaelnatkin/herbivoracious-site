<em>I've been traveling this week, so I thought I'd take the opportunity to share one of my favorite recipes from {{% product id="herbivoracious-cookbook" target="_blank" %}}the Herbivoracious cookbook{{% /product %}}. I saw some beautiful maitake mushrooms at Pike Place Market recently, so this should be a good time of year to seek them out and try this soup.</em>

Maitakes, also known as hen-of-the-woods (not to be confused with chicken-of-the-woods, which is completely different!), are a wild mushroom well worth seeking out at farmers’ markets or gourmet grocers. Maitakes are rather expensive, and their dramatic ruffled appearance and rich flavor are unusual, so when I use them, I like to make them the focus of a dish.

If you have never had {{% product id="taylors-of-harrogate-lapsang-souchong-loose" %}}lapsang souchong tea{{% /product %}}, you are in for a treat. It is intensely smoky, unlike any other tea I’ve had. That smokiness makes it a perfect broth base to show off the earthiness of roasted maitakes.

You could cut the maitake up into bite-sized pieces, but this dish is more dramatic when the mushroom is served in larger pieces—which also keeps it from getting soggy. Give guests a knife so they can cut the maitake at the last moment.

  <h2 class="recipe-card-title">Roasted Maitake Mushrooms In Smoky Tea Broth – Recipe</h2>
  <div class="recipe-card-meta">
    <span>Prep: 10 min</span> | <span>Cook: 20 min</span>

  <h3>Ingredients</h3>
  <ul>
  <li>1/2 cup finely diced bok choy stems</li>
  <li>1 tablespoon water</li>
  <li>1 pound fresh maitake mushroom(s)</li>
  <li>3 tablespoons vegetable oil</li>
  <li>1/2 teaspoon kosher salt</li>
  <li>2 teaspoons lapsang souchong tea</li>
  <li>2 cups boiling water</li>
  <li>1 tablespoon {{% product id="tamari" %}}tamari{{% /product %}}</li>
  <li>{{% product id="toasted-sesame-oil" %}}Toasted sesame oil{{% /product %}}</li>
  <li>{{% product id="maldon-salt-sea-salt-flakes-8-5-oz" %}}Flaky sea salt{{% /product %}} (such as Maldon)</li>
  <li>2 teaspoons finely sliced scallion, white and light green parts only</li>
</ul>
  <h3>Instructions</h3>
  <ol>
  <li>Preheat the oven to 450°F using convection, or 475°F without convection. Warm four shallow soup bowls.</li>
  <li>Place the bok choy in a small bowl with 1 tablespoon water and microwave on High for 1 minute. (Alternatively, you can steam the bok choy or blanch it in boiling water.) Reserve.</li>
  <li>Divide the mushroom into 4 portions (leaving the pieces as large as possible) and place on a rimmed baking sheet. Toss with the vegetable oil, coating the mushroom as evenly as possible, and sprinkle with 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt. Roast until fragrant, starting to brown, and becoming tender, about 20 minutes.</li>
  <li>While the mushroom is roasting, place the lapsang souchong tea in a small, heatproof bowl and cover with the boiling water; allow to steep 5 minutes. Strain. Add the tamari. Taste and season with a bit of salt if needed. The broth should be smoky and mildly salty, with a distinct note of soy.</li>
  <li>When the mushrooms are ready, place one portion in each bowl. Place the bok choy dice around the maitake. Reheat the tea in the microwave and divide among the 4 bowls. Garnish each bowl with a drizzle of toasted sesame oil, a pinch of flaky sea salt, and the scallions. Serve piping hot.</li>
</ol>

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is maitake?</h3>
<p>Maitakes, also known as hen-of-the-woods, are a wild mushroom with a dramatic ruffled appearance and rich flavor, well worth seeking out at farmers' markets or gourmet grocers. They are not to be confused with chicken-of-the-woods, which is completely different.</p>

<h3>What is lapsang souchong tea?</h3>
<p>An intensely smoky tea, unlike any other. That smokiness makes it a perfect broth base to show off the earthiness of roasted maitakes.</p>

<h3>Why leave the maitake in large pieces?</h3>
<p>The dish is more dramatic with the mushroom served in larger pieces, which also keeps it from getting soggy. Give guests a knife so they can cut the maitake at the last moment.</p>

<h3>How do you cook the maitake?</h3>
<p>Divide the mushroom into 4 portions, toss with vegetable oil and salt, and roast on a rimmed baking sheet in a 450 degree F convection oven (475 degrees without convection).</p>
