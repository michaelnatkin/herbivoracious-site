<p><a rel="lightbox" href="/images/2008/08/04/teasmokedlychee.webp"><img width="455" height="302" border="0" src="/images/2008-small/08/04/teasmokedlychee.webp" title="Fresh lychee smoked with genmaicha tea" alt="Fresh lychee smoked with genmaicha tea" /></a><br /><span style="font-size: 0.8em;">Fresh lychee smoked with genmaicha tea<br /></span></p>  <p>This is the first of three <a target="_blank" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amuse_bouche">amuse bouche</a> I have in mind based on fresh lychees. (Hey, did you know that the plural of amuse bouche is amuse bouche? Learn something every day.) You could also serve all three as an appetizer trio instead of an amuse.</p>  <p>If you haven't had a fresh lychee, seek them out at a good Asian grocery or a high-end produce shop. I've even seen them at Trader Joe's. They are miles beyond the fairly sad canned fruit that sometimes appears in perfunctory desserts at Chinese restaurants.<br /> </p>  <p>Without a doubt the best use of these gems is to eat them out of hand, lazily peeling one at a time on a hot day. The flavor is quite sweet, but with a perfumed and slightly smoky note. Inside is a fascinatingly smooth pit (like the inner bark of a madrone/madrona tree for those of you on the west coast of the US). </p>  <p>The flesh of the lychee is, well, fleshy, which is what inspired this series of bites. It made me think that they could stand up to a fairly intense treatment while retaining their character. Since the fruit is Asian, I wanted to go with Asian flavors in a format like an omnivore might do with a mussel or scallop.</p>    <p>For the first amuse, I cold-smoked the lychee with <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genmaicha" target="_blank">genmaicha</a> tea (a green tea with toasted brown rice), then served it cold with a broth of the same tea, a bit of grated ginger, and a couple microgreen leaves. Ideally the leaves would be micro-shiso, but I only had a mixed container and it is pretty hard to tell which is which!</p>  <div class="recipe-card">

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How do you smoke lychee with tea?</h3>
<p>Put 3 tablespoons of genmaicha tea in the bottom of a small pot, set the lychee halves on a rack or inverted metal cup inside, cover and heat on the lowest setting. After about 5 minutes check that there is smoke, raising the heat a bit if not, and smoke for about 10 minutes. Then chill thoroughly.</p>

<h3>What is genmaicha?</h3>
<p>A green tea with toasted brown rice.</p>

<h3>How do you pit a fresh lychee?</h3>
<p>Peel it, split the fruit in half with a paring knife and carefully remove the pit. It is a little tricky, so you'll probably muck up a few; you only need four good halves.</p>

<h3>Where can I buy fresh lychees?</h3>
<p>At a good Asian grocery or a high-end produce shop; the author has even seen them at Trader Joe's. They are far better than canned.</p>

<h3>How do I make the broth?</h3>
<p>Brew the remaining tablespoon of tea with a cup of boiling water for a few minutes, then strain and chill.</p>
