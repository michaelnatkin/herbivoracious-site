<p>Indian pickles are not very well known in America, but they are served as part of just about any meal in their home country. A teaspoon or two of these intense pickles, which can be made with anything from lime to garlic put up in mustard oil and lots of spices, adds a great degree of interest to even the simplest bowl of rice and dal, or any Indian dish like the <a href="/2011/07/chana-mushroom-masala-recipe/">Chana Mushroom Masala</a>.</p>
<p>When I had half a can of Sichuan preserved vegetable left from making <a href="/2011/09/sichuan-dry-fried-green-beans-and-tofu-recipe/">Sichuan Dry-Fried Green Beans and Tofu</a>, it occurred to me that it wouldn’t take much to convert those vegetables into a condiment that would be at home with Indian food. The result is piquant and delicious, and it takes only 5 minutes of work.</p>

<div class="recipe-card">
<p><strong>Indian-Sichuan Pickle</strong>
<br /><em>Vegan / gluten-free / Makes 1 cup / 5 minutes</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>1 tablespoon mustard oil or vegetable oil</li>
<li>1 tablespoon black mustard seeds</li>
<li>1 teaspoon cumin seeds</li>
<li>1 teaspoon fennel seeds</li>
<li>1 cup coarsely chopped {{% product id="yuquan-sichuan-zha-cai-preserved-vegetable" %}}Sichuan preserved vegetable{{% /product %}} (about 2 whole knobs)</li>
</ul>
<ol>
<li>Heat the vegetable oil in a small skillet over a medium high flame. Add the mustard seeds, cumin seeds and fennel seeds and cook for about 1 minute, until the mustard seeds start popping and everything is fragrant but not burning.</li>
<li>Toss the seeds and oil with the preserved vegetable. You can serve it right away or keep in the refrigerator for several days.</li>
</ol>
</div>
