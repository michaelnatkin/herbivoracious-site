<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is Guilin chili sauce?</h3>
<p>A famous chili sauce from Guilin, a city in southern China, where it is considered one of the city's three treasures along with a rice liquor and pickled tofu. It contains fermented soybeans combined with a very hot, fruity chili pepper.</p>

<h3>What can I substitute for Guilin chili sauce?</h3>
<p>For a rough approximation, use half regular black bean paste and half red chili paste such as sambal oelek or sriracha, with some garlic and a bit of sugar.</p>

<h3>Where do I find Guilin chili sauce?</h3>
<p>The author uses the Lee Kum Kee brand, available at Uwajimaya in Seattle or on Amazon.</p>

<h3>How do you get the tofu well browned?</h3>
<p>Heat the oil in a wok or large skillet over a very high flame, toss in 1 pound of patted-dry extra-firm tofu and fry, tossing occasionally, until well browned on all sides. Remove to paper towels leaving as much oil behind as possible.</p>

<h3>How hot is the dish?</h3>
<p>Use 2 tablespoons of sauce if you like it 3-stars hot, and more if you are a 5-star person.</p>

<h3>Is this recipe gluten-free?</h3>
<p>It is vegetarian and vegan, but the sauce is not gluten-free.</p>
