<span style="font-size: 0.8em;">Syrian Style Labneh (Spiced Yogurt Cheese)</span>

This recipe is adapted from Poopa Dweck's  cookbook, {{% product id="aromas-of-aleppo-the-legendary-cuisine-of" %}}Aromas of Aleppo, The Legendary Cuisine of Syrian Jews.{{% /product %}} I wrote more about that book in <a href="/2008/04/recipe-syrian-y/">this post on Syrian Red Lentil Soup</a>.

Labneh, aka Lebneh, is eaten all over the Middle East. The term can cover a wide range of yogurt-based products from very thick liquid all the way through to a firmly pressed fresh cheese. It is made by draining the liquid from plain yogurt through cheesecloth, and then optionally adding flavorings, and it is quite easy to do at home.

You'll get the best results if you start with a good yogurt, such as Fage or Greek Gods. I made this batch with full fat, but I think 2% will be fine too. If you try it with non-fat, let me know how it turns out.

Dweck flavors her version with cumin, dried mint and {{% product id="aleppo-pepper-2" %}}Aleppo Pepper{{% /product %}}. She doesn't specify, but I used spearmint and it worked really well. If you don't have the Aleppo Pepper, red chili flakes would be a decent substitute. Serve this with wedges of toasted pita bread and you've got a delicious snack.

You can easily vary the flavorings with other Middle Eastern herbs and spices, citrus zest, garlic and so on. If you feel compelled to make sun-dried tomato Thai Curry labneh, please, don't tell me!

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is labneh?</h3>
<p>Labneh, also spelled lebneh, is eaten all over the Middle East. The name covers yogurt-based products from very thick liquid to a firmly pressed fresh cheese. It's made by draining the liquid from plain yogurt through cheesecloth and optionally adding flavorings.</p>

<h3>What yogurt should I use?</h3>
<p>A good one, such as Fage or Greek Gods. The author used full fat, thinks 2% will be fine too, and hasn't tried non-fat. Use 1 quart of thick Greek-style yogurt or 1.5 quarts of thinner natural yogurt.</p>

<h3>How do I drain the yogurt?</h3>
<p>Line a colander with a double layer of cheesecloth set in a bowl, pour in the yogurt and tie the cheesecloth into a ball, ideally suspended from a wooden spoon over the colander rim so its weight helps it drain. Refrigerate overnight with enough room in the bowl to catch the liquid.</p>

<h3>How firm should the labneh be?</h3>
<p>Firm enough to form somewhat decent balls. If not, drain it more.</p>

<h3>How do I serve it?</h3>
<p>Mix in the cumin, spearmint, pepper and salt, form balls a bit smaller than a golf ball with greased hands, arrange in a shallow bowl with about 1/8 inch of olive oil, and garnish with parsley or cilantro. Serve with toasted pita.</p>

<h3>What can I use instead of Aleppo pepper?</h3>
<p>Red chili flakes are a decent substitute.</p>
