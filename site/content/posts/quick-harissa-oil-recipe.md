<p>Harissa is the classic spice paste of North Africa, used in stews, as a rub, and as a condiment at the table. Oil infused with harissa is terrific for grilling vegetables, and I also like it drizzled on soups, such as the Red Lentil and Kabocha Squash soup on . You can also use a small amount of this oil to spice up a vinaigrette for dressing salads or dipping bread.</p>
<p>If you happen to have harissa paste, you can make harissa oil by simply pureeing the paste with olive oil and straining. If not, you can make a quick approximation using the recipe below. It will keep in the refrigerator for a few days.</p>
<p>Sumac is an intensely maroon powder with a mild tangy flavor. Look for it at a Middle Eastern market or online retailer.</p>

<div class="recipe-card">
<p><strong>Quick Harissa Oil</strong>
<br /><em>Vegan and gluten-free / Yields about 5 tablespoons / 15 minutes</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>½ cup extra-virgin olive oil</li>
<li>2 teaspoons Sriracha or other chile paste</li>
<li>1 teaspoon chile flakes</li>
<li>½ teaspoon ground cumin</li>
<li>½ teaspoon caraway seeds</li>
<li>½ teaspoon coriander seeds</li>
<li>2 teaspoons {{% product id="the-spice-way-pure-ground-sumac-spice-4-oz" %}}sumac powder{{% /product %}}</li>
<li>1 clove garlic, minced</li>
</ul>
<ol>
<li>Place all ingredients in a small saucepan over low heat for about 5 minutes. Do not allow the spices to sizzle; we don’t want them to cook, just to get warm enough for their flavors to infuse the oil quickly. Remove from heat and let sit for 5 more minutes.</li>
<li>Puree all of the ingredients with a stick blender or a mini-food processor. Strain through a fine sieve and the oil is ready to use.</li>
</ol>

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is harissa oil?</h3>
<p>Harissa is the classic spice paste of North Africa, used in stews, as a rub and as a table condiment. Harissa oil is olive oil infused with those flavors, which is terrific for grilling vegetables.</p>

<h3>How do you use harissa oil?</h3>
<p>Drizzle it on soups such as red lentil and kabocha squash soup, use it on grilled vegetables, or use a small amount to spice up a vinaigrette or for dipping bread.</p>

<h3>How do you make harissa oil?</h3>
<p>If you have harissa paste, puree it with olive oil and strain. If not, warm olive oil with Sriracha, chile flakes, cumin, caraway, coriander, sumac and garlic over low heat for about 5 minutes without letting the spices sizzle, then puree and strain through a fine sieve.</p>

<h3>How long does it keep, and what is sumac?</h3>
<p>It keeps in the refrigerator for a few days, and the recipe makes about 5 tablespoons. Sumac is a bright red powder with a mild tangy flavor, found at Middle Eastern markets or online.</p>
