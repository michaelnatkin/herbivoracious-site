<span style="font-size: 0.8em;">Urad Dal Patties (Vada) with Black Pepper</span>

Vada (also spelled Bada) are a species of Indian fritter or cake made with various types of lentils (dals). This particular dish is made with urad dal, which is readily available at any Indian market. While you are there, stock up on chana dal, toor dal, moong dal and all the other delicious pulses that aren't used enough in Western cuisine. If you can't find them locally, you can also {{% product id="order-them-from-amazon" %}}order them from Amazon{{% /product %}}.

The basic technique for making vada is to soak the dal for a few hours, blend it into a coarse paste with spices, and then shallow fry. They can be made in various shapes, but here I just made simple patties.

The recipe I followed comes from the wonderful (and freshly reprinted) {{% product id="the-art-of-indian-vegetarian-cooking" %}}Lord Krishna's Cuisine: The Art of Indian Vegetarian Cooking{{% /product %}} by Yamuna Devi, which is a wonderfully written, 800 page tome of the food favored by the Hare Krishna (<a href="http://www.iskcon.com/index.html">ISKCON</a>) community. Devi (born Joan Campanella) spent 8 years as the personal cook for <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhupada">Srila Prabhupada</a>. The book includes traditional foods from all over India, but they are generally on the mild side. The version below has the spices amped up to suit my pathetically overstimulated palate.

I served these vada with red quinoa which you can barely see peeking out from under the roasted cauliflower and caramelized onions, a bit of creme fraiche (but yogurt would be good too), and a quick grilled half of a Meyer lemon. On balance, yeah, this plate has a lot of brown to golden-brown hues! But it was just a quickie dinner, whaddaya want, Picasso?

<ul>
	<li>3/4 c. urad dal (without skins), available at any Indian grocery</li>
	<li>2 tsp. coarse freshly ground black pepper</li>
	<li>1/2 tsp. {{% product id="rani-asafetida-hing-ground-3-75oz" %}}asafetida powder{{% /product %}} or 1 clove garlic, minced</li>
	<li>1 tsp. cayenne pepper</li>
	<li>2 tsp. kosher salt</li>
	<li>oil for frying (or ghee)</li>
	<li>garnish: {{% product id="maldon-salt-sea-salt-flakes-8-5-oz" %}}Maldon (aka the world's greatest salt){{% /product %}} or other finishing salt, creme fraiche or yogurt (optional)</li>
</ul>
<ol>
	<li>Pick over the dal to remove any foreign material, and rinse thorougly. Cover with plenty of water and leave to soak for 4-6 hours.</li>
	<li><strong>Drain the dal.</strong> In a blender, grind half of the dal with 6 tbsp. of water (or maybe less, see comment thread below) until thoroughly smooth and fluffy, stopping to scrape down the sides as needed. Remove from blender.</li>
	<li>Put the other half of the dal in the blender with 5 tbsp. of water (or maybe less, see comment thread below) and grind briefly, until it is a coarse paste but retains some texture. Add to the first batch of dal in a bowl.</li>
	<li>Mix in the pepper, asafetida, cayenne and kosher salt. Taste and adjust seasoning.</li>
	<li>Heat a frying pan with enough oil or ghee to fill about 1/2" deep to a temperature of about 340 F.</li>
	<li>Carefully drop in portions of the dal mixture and lightly press with a spatula to form patties. About 1/4 c. will do fine.  Put as many as you can in your pan without crowding them to close. Fry until golden brown on one side (maybe 4 minutes), then flip and brown the other side. Remove from the pan, drain on a paper towel, and hit it with some finishing salt. Hold hot while you fry the rest and serve them forth. Like anything fried, they are at their best minutes after leaving the oil and deteriorate rapidly, so be ready to chow!</li>
</ol>

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What is vada?</h3>
<p>Vada (also spelled bada) is a kind of Indian fritter or cake made with various types of lentils (dals). This version is made with urad dal and simply shaped into patties.</p>

<h3>How do you make urad dal vada?</h3>
<p>Soak the dal for 4 to 6 hours, blend it into a coarse paste with the spices (black pepper, asafetida, cayenne and salt), and shallow fry. The recipe makes about 12 patties, and 2 or 3 make a nice serving.</p>

<h3>Where do I buy urad dal?</h3>
<p>At any Indian market. While you are there, stock up on chana dal, toor dal, moong dal and the other pulses that are not used enough in Western cooking.</p>

<h3>Is it vegan?</h3>
<p>It is vegetarian, and vegan as long as you do not garnish with dairy.</p>
