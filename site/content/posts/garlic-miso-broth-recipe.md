<p>Every culture has a soup to which it attributes magical healing powers. I find this broth incredibly restorative when I’m coming back from a cold, or just want something quick and deeply warming. If you want to make it into some more substantial, add a few deep fried puffs of tofu (available at Asian groceries) or thinly sliced vegetables. Personally, I prefer the austere but fortifying broth.</p>
<p>I always have a couple of kinds of miso in the refrigerator. Sealed airtight, it lasts for a very long time and is standing by when I want it for soup or to add deep flavor to a sauce.</p>
<p>A whole head of garlic sounds like a lot, but because it is simmered instead of fried, the flavor mellows rapidly into a sweetness that blends easily with the flavor of miso. I like to really taste the garlic, so I keep the amount of miso on the low side, but you could also boost the miso up to 2 or even 3 tablespoons.</p>

<div class="recipe-card">
<p><strong>Garlic Miso Broth</strong>
<br /><em>Vegan / 4 small or 2 large servings / 15 minutes</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>1 head garlic (about 10 cloves), peeled and thinly sliced</li>
<li>1 tablespoon red miso</li>
<li>1 teaspoon tamari or shoyu (high quality soy sauce)</li>
<li>1 teaspoon Chinese toasted sesame oil</li>
<li>Kosher salt</li>
<li>1 tablespoon thinly sliced green onion</li>
<li>optional: 16 deep-fried tofu puffs (room temperature)</li>
</ul>
<ol>
<li>Bring 4 cups of water to a simmer. Add the garlic and simmer for ten minutes.</li>
<li>Whisk the miso, tamari and sesame oil with a few tablespoons of water until it forms a pourable paste. Turn off the heat on the garlic broth and whisk the {{% product id="brown-miso" %}}miso paste{{% /product %}} into it.</li>
<li>Strain the soup to remove the garlic and any undissolved bits of miso. Return to the pot and reheat but do not boil. Taste and add more salt or soy sauce if needed.</li>
<li>Ladle into serving bowls, add the tofu puffs if using, sprinkle on a few green onion slices and serve immediately.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>What is garlic miso broth?</h3>
<p>A quick, restorative broth of a whole head of garlic simmered in water, then whisked with red miso, tamari and toasted sesame oil. It is vegan and takes about 15 minutes.</p>
<h3>Won't a whole head of garlic be too strong?</h3>
<p>No. Because the garlic is simmered rather than fried, its flavor mellows into a sweetness that blends easily with the miso.</p>
<h3>Should I boil the broth after adding the miso?</h3>
<p>No. Turn off the heat before whisking in the miso, then reheat gently without boiling after straining.</p>
<h3>How can I make it more filling?</h3>
<p>Add deep-fried tofu puffs (available at Asian groceries) or thinly sliced vegetables, though the author prefers the austere, fortifying broth. You can also use 2 or 3 tablespoons of miso instead of 1.</p>
