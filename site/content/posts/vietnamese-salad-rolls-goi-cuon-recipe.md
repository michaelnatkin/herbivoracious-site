I've always loved Vietnamese Salad rolls (gỏi cuốn), and I've been making them off and on for decades, so I don't know why I haven't shared them with you guys before. If you haven't had them, they are cool and refreshing, with a tender but slightly chewy rice paper wrapper surrounding thin rice noodles, lettuce, herbs, and your choice of other ingredients. For today's version, I used shiso instead of lettuce for a more pronounced and complex herbal flavor.

If this is your first time making salad rolls, allow yourself a fair amount of time to prep all the ingredients and make the rolls. This isn't a weeknight dinner for a busy family with young kids kind of thing. It will probably take you a few tries to get the hang of rolling them up neatly, but don't worry, even your failures will still be delicious. This <a href="http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8CaadFo3sw0" target="_blank">YouTube video</a> is worth watching to see the right moves - you can skip to about 2:00 in to skip the cooking of the meat she's using. You could also try her more traditional peanut and hoisin dipping sauce instead of the sweet chili version below.

Two of these rolls per person is a good sized appetizer; three and you are up into light lunch territory.

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What rice paper do I need for salad rolls?</h3>
<p>The kind that says banh trang on the package and isn't flavored with anything like sesame.</p>

<h3>How do you soften the rice paper wrappers?</h3>
<p>Submerge one wrapper in a shallow pan of very hot water for just a few seconds. It will still be fairly firm when you remove it, but it continues to hydrate while you arrange the ingredients.</p>

<h3>How do you roll Vietnamese salad rolls?</h3>
<p>Layer shiso, mint and cilantro near the edge closest to you, add a small amount of rice noodles (less than you think), put cucumber and carrot in a line near the center and two pieces of tofu near the far edge. Roll around the herbs and noodles, tuck, fold in the edges like a burrito, then continue rolling with firm but not bursting pressure.</p>

<h3>Is this a weeknight recipe?</h3>
<p>Probably not for a busy family with young kids. Allow a fair amount of time to prep and roll, and expect it to take a few tries to roll neatly; even the failures will be delicious.</p>

<h3>Why use shiso?</h3>
<p>This version uses shiso instead of lettuce for a more pronounced and complex herbal flavor.</p>

<h3>What dipping sauce goes with them?</h3>
<p>A sweet chili sauce with lime, ginger, garlic, lemongrass and green onion. A more traditional peanut and hoisin sauce also works.</p>
