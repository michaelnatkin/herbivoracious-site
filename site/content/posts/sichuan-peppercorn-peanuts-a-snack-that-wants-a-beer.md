The idea for these came from a packaged Japanese snack that I find totally addictive. It is easy to make at home, and then you can adjust the ratio of heat, orange zest, salt, and the tongue-numbing zing of the Sichuan peppercorn to your own preference. These guys pretty much have "snack with a beer" written all over them. They would also be awesome tossed with crispy, pan-fried tofu for a spicy variation on kung pao.

{{% product id="sichuan-peppercorns" %}}Sichuan peppecorns{{% /product %}} are the seed hulls (pericarps) from a tree in the citrus family. They do have a citrusy flavor, but their most prominent feature is the way they shock your tongue kind of like licking a 9-volt battery, but in a good way. The quantity I've specified in the recipe isn't large, feel free to amp it up if you find you like that zing.

You can grind the spices in a mortar and pestle, with an electric {{% product id="spice-grinder" %}}coffee grinder{{% /product %}}, or with a ceramic hand mill like this Hario model. The Hario is great because it lets you adjust the grind very precisely.

Add the peanuts and cook, tossing frequently, for 2 more minutes. Remove to the paper towels, including all of the spices. Allow to drain for a few minutes, then transfer to a bowl, toss with the salt, adjust seasoning, and serve (or they will keep for a few days).</span></li>
</ol>

<div class="faq"><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What do Sichuan peppercorns taste like?</h3>
<p>They are the seed hulls of a tree in the citrus family, so they have a citrusy flavor, but their most prominent feature is a tongue-numbing zing, a bit like licking a 9-volt battery, in a good way.</p>

<h3>How do you grind Sichuan peppercorns?</h3>
<p>Use a mortar and pestle, an electric coffee grinder, or a ceramic hand mill such as a Hario, which lets you adjust the grind very precisely.</p>

<h3>Can I make the peanuts hotter or more numbing?</h3>
<p>Yes. Adjust the ratio of heat, orange zest, salt and Sichuan peppercorn to taste. The amount in the recipe isn't large, so feel free to amp up the peppercorn if you like the zing.</p>

<h3>How long do Sichuan peppercorn peanuts keep?</h3>
<p>A few days. Drain them on paper towels, toss with the salt, and store them for snacking.</p>

<h3>How else can I use them?</h3>
<p>As a snack with a beer, or tossed with crispy, pan-fried tofu for a spicy variation on kung pao.</p>
